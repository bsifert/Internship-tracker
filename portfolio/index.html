SHEET 1 OF 5 · FALL 2025

Autonomous Mission Robot

An eight-person team built a small autonomous vehicle that drives through an obstacle course, finds the one plot of soil a seed can grow in, plants a lima bean in it, and carries a sample of the other soil to the finish. I led the team and did the CAD for the vehicle and its mission mechanism.

Role
Team leader, CAD lead
Course
ENES100, Intro to Engineering Design
Team
8 students (“Seed Squad”)
Tools
Onshape, 3D printing, shop tools

The mission

Navigate to the mission site, work out which of the plots holds plantable soil, plant a lima bean at least 10 mm deep, collect at least 10 g of the unplantable soil, and drive on to the finish under a limbo bar. The whole vehicle had to stay under 3 kg and $320.

What I did

  • Designed the vehicle in Onshape and produced the assembly and subsystem drawings the team built from.
  • Sized the chassis for weight and fit. A ¼-inch plywood base, 7 × 8 in, comes to about 125 g against a 200 g budget. The 203 × 178 mm footprint stays well under the 250 mm limit we set so it could squeeze between obstacles.
  • Designed the mission arm. Our first concepts were cranes and helical screws, which were too complex to build. We switched to a parallel four-bar linkage. It keeps the bucket level as it swings down, so one servo gives both reach and depth. A second, small servo tilts the bucket to dig, and the seed rides in a pocket at the back of the bucket.
  • Balanced the vehicle. Most of the mass sits in the arm at the front, so I placed the battery at the rear to keep the center of gravity low and central and stop the robot tipping during the dig.
  • Built it. I cut and machined the chassis with hand and power tools, designed and 3D-printed the PLA arm mount and eight motor mounts, helped assemble the drivetrain, and built the team’s practice mission site.
  • Redesigned after a failure. In testing the scooping mechanism didn’t pick up a reliable sample, so we replaced it with an adhesive-based retrieval system.

Key numbers

Mass limit3 kg
Budget limit$320
Chassis base7 × 8 × ¼ in ply, ≈125 g
Footprint≈203 × 178 mm
Arm servo40 kg·cm, 180°
Bucket servo1.5 kg·cm, 270°, 9 g
Drive4 × DC motors, mecanum wheels

Working across sub-teams. The arm had to leave the camera a clear view of the plots and fit the navigation team’s approach path. The camera reads each plot’s average color to find the plantable one, and mecanum wheels let the robot slide sideways from one plot to the next without turning.

Running the team. I wrote our team charter and built the project-management sheet that tracked every milestone’s tasks, owners and dates across the semester.

The work

  • Help design, build and test the suspension each season using SolidWorks, finite element analysis, a range of production methods, and prototyping.
  • Work toward better driver steering, stability and feedback, which is the sub-team’s goal for the car.

Technical brief: anti-squat and anti-dive

In September 2026 I prepared a short presentation for the sub-team on how suspension geometry keeps the car level under throttle and brakes.

  • Anti-dive (front, braking) stops the nose from dropping when the driver brakes. It’s set by angling the front arms in side view.
  • Anti-squat (rear, acceleration) stops the tail from dropping on throttle. It’s set by angling the rear arms.
  • Why it matters: less pitch keeps ride height and ground clearance steady, holds camber on target so the tires keep their contact patch, and makes the car respond faster because load goes through the links right away.
  • The trade-off: too much makes the car harsh over bumps and dulls what the driver feels.

The problem

My internship search lived in a Google Sheet with 85 companies, each with a research status, priority, deadline, contacts and notes. The sheet could store all of it but couldn’t tell me what to do next. Deadlines slipped past, and follow-ups were easy to forget.

What I built

  • “Do these next” ranking. Every company is scored on overdue deadlines, follow-ups that are due, open postings and postings about to open, weighted by priority. The most pressing item is always at the top.
  • Urgency board and table. A board groups companies by research status. A sortable, filterable table supports multi-select bulk edits.
  • Application history. Every status change is timestamped, so each company has a timeline from first look to offer.
  • Spreadsheet round-trip. Exports to JSON or CSV. Importing a CSV from Google Sheets merges by company name: it updates existing rows, adds new ones, and leaves out-of-file columns alone.
  • No account needed. Data autosaves in the browser, and the app reminds me to download a backup when the last one gets old.

Built with

  • TypeScript
  • React 19
  • Vite
  • Tailwind CSS
  • GitHub Actions
  • GitHub Pages

Safe upgrades for saved data. New fields like posting links and follow-up dates were added after the app was already in use. Every saved record is normalized when it loads, so older saves never break the newer version.

Automated deploys. A GitHub Actions workflow type-checks, builds and publishes the site on every push.
