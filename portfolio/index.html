Minimum-Mass Titanium Bell Crank

Designed a suspension bell crank for a Terps Racing mass-reduction challenge. Took it from a 38.87 g blank to 8.85 g in 0.125 in titanium while meeting every strength, stiffness and buckling requirement.

Requirements

Max deflection≤ 0.1 mm
Stock0.125 in Ti-5Al-2.5Sn, waterjet cut
Min. feature0.75 mm
Ring around holes≥ 1.5 mm (my own rule, to protect the bores)

Outcome

Widening the search from constant-width legs to tapered, offset legs with individual fillets cut a further 16%, from 10.54 g to 8.85 g. Compression stress, tension stress, buckling and deflection now all sit at their limits at the same time, so this shape can’t get lighter without breaking a requirement.

Approach

  1. Set up the studiesThree SolidWorks Simulation studies (max compression, max tension, buckling) with the pivot fixed and a 0.225 mm mesh control at the hot spots.
  2. Parameterized the shapeA triangular frame with 18 variables: width at each end of every leg, leg offsets, boss radii, pocket-corner radii and individual fillets.
  3. Optimized for massAn AI-assisted Python optimization with a plane-stress FEA model that checked stress, deflection and buckling at every step.
  4. Removed mesh noiseTiny geometry changes were causing 0.5–1.5% jumps in peak stress. A 0.12 mm mesh on fillets and pocket corners fixed it.
  5. Compared layoutsA braced, topology-style frame was heavier than a plain triangle at the same safety factor, so the triangle was kept.
  6. Verified independentlyCross-checked with a 3D quadratic-tetrahedral model (buckling FOS 1.80 vs. 1.78), then confirmed the final geometry in SolidWorks.

Manufacturing check. Margins are thin: deflection is 1.6% under its limit and FOS is 0.6% over. A 0.1 mm waterjet over-cut would take about 4% off the thinnest leg, so I recommended toolpath compensation or adding 0.1 mm to each leg, which costs about 0.2 g.
