Minimum-Mass Titanium Bell Crank

Designed a suspension bell crank for a mass-reduction design challenge. Cut the part from a 38.87 g blank to 8.85 g (77% lighter) in 0.125 in Ti-5Al-2.5Sn, while meeting every strength, stiffness and buckling requirement.

Starting blank38.87 g
First optimized design10.54 g
Final design8.85 g (−77%)

Requirements

  • Factor of safety of at least 1.75 in both the max compression and max tension load cases, and in buckling.
  • Maximum deflection of 0.1 mm.
  • Waterjet-cut from 0.125 in Ti-5Al-2.5Sn, with no feature narrower than 0.75 mm.
  • My own added rule: at least 1.5 mm of material around each hole, to protect the bearing and pin bores.

Approach

  • Set up three SolidWorks Simulation studies (max compression, max tension, buckling) with the pivot fixed and a 0.225 mm mesh control on the stress hot spots.
  • Defined the part as a triangular frame with 18 design variables: width at each end of every leg, leg offsets, boss radii, pocket-corner radii and individual outer fillets.
  • Used an AI-assisted Python optimization with a plane-stress FEA model that checked stress, deflection and plate and in-plane buckling at every step.
  • Found that tiny geometry changes were causing 0.5–1.5% jumps in peak stress. Traced it to mesh noise and fixed it with a 0.12 mm mesh on fillets and pocket corners.
  • Compared layouts. A braced, topology-style frame was heavier than a plain triangle at the same safety factor, so the triangle was kept.
  • Cross-checked deflection and buckling with an independent 3D quadratic-tetrahedral model (buckling FOS 1.80 vs. 1.78), then verified the final geometry in SolidWorks.

Outcome

Widening the search from constant-width legs to tapered, offset legs with individual fillets took the design from 10.54 g to 8.85 g, a further 16%. At the final design, compression stress, tension stress, buckling and deflection all sit at their limits at the same time, so this shape can’t get lighter without breaking a requirement.

Manufacturing check

Margins are thin: deflection is 1.6% under its limit and FOS is 0.6% over. A 0.1 mm waterjet over-cut would take about 4% off the thinnest leg, so I recommended toolpath compensation or adding 0.1 mm to each leg (about 0.2 g).
